# AI for business problems: scale without firing people

![Human helmsman and crew on a powerful sailing ship](https://dxggowrfyirnanabowhr.supabase.co/storage/v1/object/public/site-images/site-ai-for-business-problems-scale-without-firing-people-1792891274.jpg)

I used to have inflated expectations of AI too. Social media ads make it sound easy: click a few buttons, spin up a hundred agents, and let them manage your business. Watching enough of those videos can make the promise feel quite ordinary

I went and tried it myself. That is how I found the gap between the advertising and the work. A human still stands behind the AI, making choices and operating it

## Scaling does not remove the need for judgment

AI can help a business handle a larger workload. Processing additional orders, speeding up parts of a workflow, and improving reporting or routine finance and accounting work are useful applications. Firing everyone because of that would be a terrible move right now

Employees bring experience. Someone has to distinguish a good piece of work from an average one. Having more output does not supply that judgment, and faster processing does not make the quality question disappear

![Team reviewing documents](https://images.pexels.com/photos/7693692/pexels-photo-7693692.jpeg)

Consider the difference between preparing a report and deciding what to do with it. Preparation can be part of the routine workload. Assessing the result still calls for someone who understands the work. Those responsibilities do not become identical just because preparation takes less time

## Repetition is a better starting point

Routine operations are well suited to AI automation. It makes sense to look for repetitive work within order handling, reporting and accounting processes. That is a different proposition from handing over every responsibility of an entire department

In my experience, optimization gets harder as a task becomes more complex, more intellectually demanding and made up of more parts. A promise to run the whole company packages all those different responsibilities into a single attractive sentence

![Colleagues discussing charts](https://images.pexels.com/photos/4990545/pexels-photo-4990545.jpeg)

The useful distinction is between an operation that can be automated and the human decision made from its result. My [AI consulting](https://matintellect.com/en/services/ai-consulting) work addresses that distinction: which routine work fits automation, and where experienced people remain necessary

This is why I look at AI through the lens of scale. The workload can grow while people continue to assess what is produced. Their experience is part of the process even when some of the repetitive preparation becomes faster

I want people to approach artificial intelligence thoughtfully and use it in ways that genuinely help. Trying it yourself is more informative than relying on the promise of a business run by a hundred agents. More to come...
