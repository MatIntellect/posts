# AI content factory vs content manager: decision guide and role split

Type: decision guide with a responsibility table. Reader: an owner, marketing lead or developer deciding how to staff content work across several platforms. Goal: choose between a content manager alone, a factory alone or a manager with a factory, and define who does what in the combined setup. Limits: guidance from my own practice; cost figures are for you to fill in, not benchmarks

![AI-generated illustration of an AI content factory vs a content manager](https://dxggowrfyirnanabowhr.supabase.co/storage/v1/object/public/site-images/site-ai-content-factory-vs-content-manager-1793764684.jpg)

## Definitions

Content manager: a person who discusses topics with the team, prepares material and publishes

Content factory: a configured AI process that finds topics, gathers sources, writes in the author's style and adapts text to each platform; a person chooses topics and approves results

Reference: my own factory watches 13 sources around the clock, checks facts before drafting, and prepares versions for Telegram and the website in Russian and English plus Facebook, Instagram, Threads and YouTube, each approved separately. About an hour per post by hand became about 5 minutes to a preview; 84 posts published, none without approval. Turnkey setup takes about a week

## Decision table

| Situation | Recommendation | Why |
|---|---|---|
| One platform, occasional posts | Manager | Setup would not pay off |
| Several platforms, same post rewritten for each | Factory with a reviewer | Routine grows with each platform |
| Manager already in place | Manager with factory | Manager keeps topics, voice and review; factory takes repetition |
| Nobody available to approve | Neither yet | A factory without review produces fast mistakes |
| Business has no clear message | Work on the message first | A factory speeds up process, not positioning |

## Measuring before deciding

Before filling the worksheet, time one ordinary post end to end: topic, sources, fact check, first version, each platform version, pictures, publishing. Repeat for two or three posts, because the first is usually slower. With a factory, time the review of each preview the same way. Decisions made on measured minutes hold up much better than decisions made on a salary compared with a subscription price

## Signals

Time to consider a factory:

- half the manager's week goes into rewriting one text for different platforms
- some platforms lag by weeks while others are active
- finding topics takes hours
- a new platform or language feels like another hire

Too early for a factory:

- one platform, rare posts
- no one to look at previews and approve
- the business does not yet know what it wants to say

## Rollout notes

Introduce the factory with the manager, not around them. Show previews and ask what they would change; their edits become the first rules. Start with drafts for one platform and compare with the manager's own versions. Widen to other platforms when the manager says the drafts are good enough

Keep publishing behind approval. Giving the factory permission to publish on its own to save minutes is the fastest way to a post you have to apologise for

Do not cut the manager after launch. Without someone choosing and checking, quality drops and the factory's speed works against you
