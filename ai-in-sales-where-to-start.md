# AI in sales: where to start

![Incoming inquiries](https://dxggowrfyirnanabowhr.supabase.co/storage/v1/object/public/site-images/site-ai-in-sales-where-to-start-1792468371.jpg)

My starting point for sales automation is the shape of the task. When incoming information is well defined, the expected output is explicit, and the sequence is short, AI has a much clearer job to do. In my view, that is where it brings the most value

Handing over every sale and every customer conversation is a different proposition. Human interactions can take too many different paths. I would expect that kind of complete handover to fail, rather than choose it as the first implementation

## Define the first task

I would begin with capturing all incoming inquiries and following their handling progress. There is no need to start by generating customer replies. The initial purpose is to stop inquiries from disappearing and keep the progress of each inquiry visible

![Sales office](https://images.pexels.com/photos/8867190/pexels-photo-8867190.jpeg)

There are two things to distinguish here. Recording that an inquiry arrived tells you it exists. Tracking its handling tells you what happens after arrival. A collection of incoming records alone does not establish that the work on those inquiries is moving forward

The input to this first task is a customer inquiry. Its output is a captured record with visible progress. This makes the scope specific enough to examine inquiry by inquiry: is the incoming item present, and is its subsequent handling visible?

## Keep conversation outside this initial scope

Capturing an inquiry does not require assigning the same system responsibility for the entire exchange with the customer. Recording information and choosing what to say in a developing conversation are separate actions. The latter introduces the range of human situations that makes a full handover risky

![Document handling](https://images.pexels.com/photos/37568245/pexels-photo-37568245.jpeg)

I explained the reasoning on my blog: [AI in sales: where to start](https://matintellect.com/en/posts/ai-in-sales-where-to-start). The starting work is ordinary systems analysis, focused on understanding what comes in and how the handling of each item can be seen

This is also why I recommend a gradual rollout. A strict repeated step from an existing process has a boundary. An instruction to manage sales independently leaves the system facing the whole variety of conversations instead of that single bounded action

## Use the same test for the next task

After inquiry capture, assess another part of the process on its own terms. Can its incoming information be specified? Is there an identifiable output? Does reaching that output involve a short sequence that repeats? Those are the properties that make the task a sensible candidate for automation

The first-stage outcome should stay equally specific. Every incoming inquiry is accounted for, and its handling progress is visible. That is a concrete place to begin without treating an initial automation task as a replacement for the sales department
