# AI tools for business: picking the right mix

![Cover](https://dxggowrfyirnanabowhr.supabase.co/storage/v1/object/public/site-images/site-ai-tools-for-business-picking-the-right-mix-1791682930.jpg)

The question I hear most often is "which AI model should our business pick". The honest answer is not a name - it is a mix. The LLM market is huge now, and tying yourself to a single model means doing half your work at half strength. My own stack combines Claude, Codex, GLM, Gemini and others, with each model holding its own job - like tools on a garage wall, one per task

![Photo](https://images.pexels.com/photos/4974915/pexels-photo-4974915.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&h=650&w=940)

## How the roles split

Claude is the wise owl: it sees ahead, which makes it excellent at strategy and the architecture of a future project - the thinking stage before anything gets built

ChatGPT is the rottweiler: it grabs a task and does not let go until it is done. I mostly deploy it as an auditor of Claude's output - one model builds, the other hunts for mistakes, and reliability climbs

GLM takes the repetitive work: it writes solid text and works as a sharp reviewer too. Gemini handles image generation in my setup, while Groq runs voice - every voice message in my system passes through it. The voice assistant runs on separate models of its own

## Skip the beauty contest

The classic startup mistake is one subscription for everything - emails, analytics, copy, code. Half the tasks come out forced, and the verdict becomes "AI does not work". Wrong tool, not wrong technology

The only honest test is your own task through two or three models: compare quality, speed, and the price of the plan as volume grows. Someone else's leaderboard says nothing about your workload

Two habits keep the mix healthy: write down what each test showed, and revisit the stack when a model or a price changes. That is the entire maintenance budget of a multi-model setup

Local models are the one thing I have not got to yet - they need serious hardware, and most businesses are fine with cloud models until strict data-privacy requirements show up

A short table of task, model and verdict is all the documentation this setup needs - skip it, and in two months you re-run tests you already paid for
